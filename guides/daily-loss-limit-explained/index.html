Daily loss limit vs max drawdown, explained

By the Adiox team · Updated 27 November 2026 · Based on Adiox's published product rules · Editorial standards · Not investment advice

A daily loss limit caps how much you can lose in one trading day; a maximum (overall) drawdown caps how far your account can fall in total. The detail that matters is what each limit is measured from — the starting balance, the day's opening balance, or your highest equity — because that decides how much room you really have.

Three ways firms measure loss

Static, daily-reset and trailing limits

Worked example

One day on a $10,000 challenge

  1. Midnight UTC

    Opening balance recorded: $10,400. With a 5% daily limit, today's floor is $9,880.

  2. Morning

    A $150 winning trade takes equity to $10,550 — the floor stays $9,880, so you have $670 of room.

  3. Afternoon

    The overall floor never moved: it is still $9,000, 10% below the $10,000 start.

FAQ

Loss limit questions

What is a daily loss limit in prop trading?

The most an account may lose in a single trading day, usually a percentage of a reference balance. On Adiox challenges it is a percentage of the day's opening balance, recorded at midnight UTC.

What is the difference between daily loss and max drawdown?

Daily loss limits one day's losses and resets each day. Max (overall) drawdown limits the total fall from a fixed or trailing reference point for the whole challenge. On Adiox the overall limit is static, measured from the starting balance.

Is a static or trailing drawdown better?

A static drawdown gives you more room as you profit, because the floor stays where it started. A trailing drawdown moves up with your highest balance, so your room never grows. Adiox challenges use a static overall limit.

Can I practise with a daily loss limit?

Yes — every Adiox Training Room simulator enforces a daily and an overall loss limit, and custom simulators let you choose the percentages.
