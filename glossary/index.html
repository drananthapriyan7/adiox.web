The Trading Glossary

Every prop trading term, explained in plain language in about sixty seconds — definition first, depth second, then where to see it live in Adiox.

🔎

Breach RULES

A breach is any violation of a prop firm's account rules — most commonly exceeding the daily loss limit or maximum drawdown. A breach typically ends the evaluation or funded account immediately, regardless of how profitable the account was before it. Some rule violations (like news-window trades) may void profits instead of terminating.

Most breaches are mechanical, not market-driven: an oversized position after losses crosses the daily limit on an otherwise survivable day. That's why breach prevention is about position sizing and hard stops on your own behavior, not better market analysis.

See it in Adiox: the Risk Manager warns before a breach, not after →

Challenge / Evaluation FUNDING

An evaluation (or "challenge") is a test on simulated capital where a trader must hit a profit target while respecting drawdown and consistency rules to qualify for a funded account. Formats include 2-step (two phases), 1-step (one phase), instant (no evaluation, tighter rules), and pay-later (no upfront fee).

The fee is the trader's only capital at risk; the firm's simulated capital absorbs trading risk. Failure rates are high — mostly on rules rather than markets — which is why rehearsing the exact rule set first is the single highest-ROI preparation.

See it in Adiox: compare all four challenge formats →

Copier (trade copier) EXECUTION

A trade copier automatically reproduces trades from one source — a strategy provider or signal feed — onto your account, scaled to your risk settings. Cloud copiers run server-side, so no VPS or always-on computer is needed; the mirror typically executes in under a second.

The critical safety feature is precedence: your allocation caps, equity floors, and per-trade risk limits should always override whatever the source does. A copier without receiver-side risk control is just outsourced gambling.

Daily loss limit RULES

The maximum an account's equity may fall below the day's starting balance before the firm ends the account or evaluation — typically 3–5%. Firms differ on two details that end careers: what time the "day" resets, and whether floating (unrealized) P&L counts toward the limit.

Professional practice is to set a personal stop 20–30% inside the firm's line and automate it, so a bad day ends with a lockout instead of a termination email.

See it in Adiox: the daily loss lock →

Drawdown RISK

Drawdown is the decline in account equity from a peak to a subsequent low, usually expressed as a percentage. Prop firms enforce a maximum overall drawdown (commonly 6–12%) alongside the daily limit; touching it ends the account. Some firms compute it from the initial balance ("static"), others from the highest equity reached ("trailing").

Knowing which calculation your firm uses matters more than the headline number — a trailing drawdown follows your profits up and can breach on a pullback that a static one survives.

See it in Adiox: feel the limit in a $10 PropSim run →

Expectancy METRICS

Expectancy is the average amount you win or lose per trade across a large sample — (win rate × average win) minus (loss rate × average loss). Positive expectancy means repeating the strategy compounds your account upward; negative means every additional trade is a donation.

It's the single most honest strategy metric because it can't be gamed by win rate alone: a 90% win rate with occasional catastrophic losers still shows its true color in expectancy.

See it in Adiox: expectancy on every backtest →

Funded account FUNDING

A funded account is what a trader receives after passing an evaluation: an account backed by the firm's (typically simulated) capital, where the trader keeps a published share of profits — commonly 70–90% — paid out on a schedule. The firm's rules (drawdown, news, consistency) continue to apply for the account's life.

"Funded" does not mean "safe": most funded accounts are lost to the same rule breaches that fail evaluations, which is why protection tooling matters most after you pass.

See it in Adiox: payout terms published upfront →

Leverage BASICS

Leverage lets you control a position larger than your account balance — 1:20 leverage means $1,000 controls $20,000 of exposure. It multiplies both gains and losses equally; the market doesn't know or care how leveraged you are, but your drawdown limit does.

In prop trading, leverage discipline is effectively position-sizing discipline: the question is never "how much can I control?" but "how much can I lose before the daily limit?"

News rule RULES

A news rule restricts trading around high-impact economic releases (NFP, CPI, FOMC). Common forms: no opening or closing positions within 2–5 minutes of the event, no holding through it, or news-window profits not counting toward targets. Violations can void profits or terminate funded accounts.

Rules vary firm by firm, and the difference between "can't open" and "can't hold" is the difference between a fine day and a flagged account — read your firm's exact wording, or automate compliance.

Pip BASICS

A pip is the standard unit of price movement in forex — for most pairs, the fourth decimal place (0.0001). If EURUSD moves from 1.0850 to 1.0851, that's one pip. Your per-pip dollar value depends on position size, which is why "it only moved 20 pips" means nothing without the lot size attached.

Thinking in pips without thinking in dollars (or better, in R-multiples) is one of the earliest habits the Academy trains out.

Profit factor METRICS

Profit factor is gross profit divided by gross loss over a set of trades. Above 1.0 the strategy makes money; 1.5+ is generally solid; 2.0+ is excellent and — in backtests — worth a skeptical second look for overfitting. It complements win rate by accounting for how big winners are relative to losers.

A 35%-win-rate trend strategy with a 2.1 profit factor is a better business than a 75%-win-rate scalper at 1.05.

See it in Adiox: profit factor on every replay →

Prop firm FUNDING

A proprietary trading firm ("prop firm") lets traders trade the firm's capital — in the modern retail model, simulated capital — after passing a performance evaluation, splitting profits with the trader. The trader risks only the evaluation fee; the firm manages its risk through drawdown rules and payout verification.

The model rewards consistency over brilliance: firms profit from disciplined traders who survive, which is why every rule ultimately encodes "don't blow up."

See it in Adiox: the professional stage →

R-multiple METRICS

An R-multiple measures a trade's result in units of its initial risk. Risk $100 with your stop, make $200 — that's +2R; lose the $100 — that's -1R. R-multiples make trades of any size honestly comparable and expose the deadliest pattern in retail trading: -3R losers hiding among +1R winners.

A journal kept in R tells you in one histogram whether your losses are controlled — dollars can't do that across changing account sizes.

See it in Adiox: your R-distribution, charted →

Revenge trading PSYCHOLOGY

Revenge trading is re-entering the market quickly after a loss, usually with increased size, trying to "win it back." Its measurable signature: entries within minutes of a losing close, position size above your average after losses, and abandonment of your normal setups. It's the single most common path from a bad hour to a breached account.

Because the signs are measurable, the fix is mechanical: cooldown timers, post-loss size caps, and an engine that flags the sequence as it starts.

Scaling plan FUNDING

A scaling plan is a prop firm's published schedule for growing a funded account after sustained performance — for example, +25–50% capital after several profitable payout cycles with no breaches, often with an improved profit split. It converts consistency into compounding without the trader adding capital.

Scaling terms differ widely; serious multi-account traders track each firm's milestones the way salespeople track quotas.

See it in Adiox: milestone tracking across every firm →

Slippage EXECUTION

Slippage is the difference between the price you requested and the price you actually got filled at. It's usually pennies in quiet markets and brutal around news events — which is one practical reason firms restrict news trading and why realistic simulators model it instead of granting perfect fills.

A strategy that only works with zero slippage doesn't work.

See it in Adiox: PropSim models real spreads and slippage →

Win rate METRICS

Win rate is the percentage of trades that close profitably. Alone, it's the most misleading statistic in trading: a 30% win rate with +3R winners is a money machine, while a 75% win rate with occasional -6R disasters is a slow-motion account failure. It only gains meaning next to average win/loss size.

Marketing that leads with win rate and hides drawdown is telling you exactly what it doesn't want you to ask.
