How Copy Trading Works

Copy trading links two accounts. The source account places a trade and the copier automatically copies it to the connected destination account.

  1. The source account places a trade

    A trade is opened on the source account, in MT5 or MT4.

  2. The copier picks it up

    The copier sees the new trade on the source account straight away.

  3. The destination gets the copy

    The copier automatically places the same trade on the connected destination account: MT5, MT4 or Adiox.

Copy trading glossary

What the terms and numbers mean

How to read a copier connection and a Top Copiers card before you copy a trader.

Source account
The account that places the trade: MT5 or MT4. The copier watches it for new trades.
Destination account
The account that receives the copy: MT5, MT4 or Adiox, depending on the connection.
Copier
The Adiox software that sees a new trade on the source account and places the same trade on the destination account.
Gain %
How much the trader's account grew over the stated window, as a percentage. Shown with the profit in money and the number of trades.
Win rate
The share of closed trades that ended in profit. Read it together with drawdown and profit factor.
Max drawdown
The largest drop from a peak in the trader's account over the window. Lower means the ride was smoother.
Profit factor
Gross profit divided by gross loss. Above 1.0 means the trader made more than they lost.
Active copiers
How many accounts copy the trader right now. It shows popularity, not quality.
Martingale warning
Marks a trader who adds to losing positions, often with a bigger size. Higher risk of a large drawdown.

How to choose a trader to copy

  • Check the connection: the trader's platform must be a supported source for your destination account.
  • Look for steady gain over the window with enough trades, not one lucky week.
  • Prefer a lower max drawdown and a profit factor above 1.
  • Be careful with a martingale warning: it means higher risk of a big drawdown.
  • Check the monthly fee and how many active copiers already follow the trader.

FAQ

Copy trading FAQ

What is copy trading on Adiox?
Copy trading links a source account to a destination account. When the source account places a trade, the Adiox copier automatically copies it to the connected destination account. You set it up in the Adiox copy trading portal at copier.adiox.io.
What is a trade copier?
A trade copier is software that watches a source trading account and automatically places the same trades on a connected destination account. The Adiox trade copier works with MT5, MT4 and Adiox accounts.
Which copier connections are supported?
Source account to destination account: MT5 to MT5, MT5 to MT4, MT4 to MT4, MT5 to Adiox and MT4 to Adiox.
Can I copy trades from MT5 to MT4?
Yes. MT5 to MT4 is a supported connection. Connect the MT5 account as the source and the MT4 account as the destination, and new MT5 trades are copied to MT4.
Can I copy trades from MT4 to MT5?
MT4 to MT5 is not one of the listed connections. From an MT4 source account you can copy to another MT4 account or to an Adiox account.
Can I copy trades from MT5 to an Adiox account?
Yes. Connect your MT5 account as the source and your Adiox account as the destination. When a trade is placed on MT5, the copier automatically copies it to the Adiox account. MT4 to Adiox works the same way.
How are the top copiers ranked?
Top copiers are shown in the ranking order returned by the Adiox copy trading API, with the highest-ranked first. Each one shows its gain and profit over the stated window, win rate, maximum drawdown, profit factor, trades and active copiers.
What does the martingale warning mean?
It marks a trader whose history shows martingale-style trading: adding to losing positions, often with a bigger size each time. It can lift returns for a while, but it raises the risk of a large drawdown.
Where do I set up copy trading?
In the Adiox copy trading portal at copier.adiox.io. Sign in, connect your source and destination accounts, and the copier starts copying new trades.
Is copy trading risk-free?
No. Copied trades carry the same market risk as the source trades, and past results don't guarantee future results. Trading leveraged products can lose money.
