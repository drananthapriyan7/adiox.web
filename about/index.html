THE ORIGIN PROBLEM

Trading tools were never designed to work together

Every trader we knew ran the same broken stack: a course in one tab, a journal in a spreadsheet, a prop firm dashboard in another window, a signal group on Telegram, a backtester somewhere else, and a Discord for competitions. None of it connected. Progress made in one tool was invisible to every other.

The cost wasn't just friction — it was money and time. Traders paid for evaluations they'd never rehearsed, subscribed to signals they couldn't verify, and journaled into spreadsheets no coach would ever read. The industry sold pieces; nobody built the system.

Adiox is that system. One account where lessons flow into practice, practice into competition, and competition into a professional record — with an AI layer watching all of it on your behalf. We didn't set out to build another tool. We set out to build the layer that makes all of them make sense.

What it is
Adiox is a unified prop trading ecosystem built around four stages: Learn, Train, Compete, Trade. Traders study with an AI coach, practice prop-firm-style evaluations in a realistic simulator, compete in tournaments and PvP trading games, and graduate to funded challenges, copy trading, signals, and professional risk tools — all inside one account.

THE PHILOSOPHY

Four stages, because evolution has an order

Knowledge before risk. Practice before payment. Proof before reputation. Tools before scale. Every product decision at Adiox is a test against that sequence — if a feature encourages skipping a stage, it doesn't ship.

THE COMPANY

A small team that trades what it builds

Adiox is built by traders, engineers, and designers who ran the broken multi-tool stack themselves. Every feature ships after internal use on our own accounts — if it doesn't survive our trading, it doesn't reach yours.

Transparency is the product

Published rules, verified records, honest statuses. In an industry of edited screenshots, receipts are our moat.

Never sell the skip

We won't market shortcuts past stages. Money spent before skill is the industry's oldest trap — we're built against it.

Data over adjectives

Specific numbers beat superlatives — in our marketing, our coaching, and our roadmap updates.
