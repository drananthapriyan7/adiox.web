METRICS THAT MATTER

The six numbers that decide if your edge is real

Win rate

The percentage of trades that close in profit. Meaningless alone — a 30% win rate with big winners beats a 70% one with big losers.

Glossary: win rate →
Profit factor

Gross profit ÷ gross loss. Above 1.0 you're profitable; 1.5+ is solid; 2.0+ deserves a check for overfitting.

Glossary: profit factor →
Expectancy

Average amount you make (or lose) per trade. The single number that says whether repeating this strategy compounds up or down.

Glossary: expectancy →
Max drawdown

Your worst peak-to-trough equity drop. If it exceeds a prop firm's limit in backtest, it will in the evaluation too.

Glossary: drawdown →
R-multiples

Every result measured in units of initial risk. A +2R trade made twice what it risked — the honest way to compare trades of any size.

Glossary: R-multiple →
Trade frequency

Setups per week at your rules. Decides whether the edge is tradable within evaluation time limits and your actual schedule.

QUESTIONS

Backtesting, answered

What is bar replay?

Bar replay plays historical market data forward candle by candle, hiding the future, so you can trade the past as if it were live. Unlike scrolling a chart — where your eye already knows the outcome — replay forces real decisions at the hard right edge, at any speed from 1x to 20x.

How much historical data do I need to backtest a strategy?

Enough to cover different market regimes: trending, ranging, and high-volatility periods. As a rule of thumb, at least 100 trades from your rules across 2+ years of data for intraday strategies, and 5–10 years for swing strategies. The Adiox engine covers 10+ years so regime variety is never the limit.

What is profit factor?

Profit factor is gross profit divided by gross loss. Above 1.0 the strategy makes money; 1.5+ is generally solid; 2.0+ is excellent but rare and worth double-checking for overfitting. It's a better health check than win rate alone because it accounts for how big your winners are relative to your losers.

Is backtesting better than demo trading?

They answer different questions. Backtesting compresses years of strategy evidence into hours — ideal for proving an edge exists. Demo (or simulated evaluation) trading tests execution and psychology in real time. The efficient order: backtest until the numbers hold up, then pressure-test yourself in PropSim before paying for an evaluation.
